## Step 5: boot socket U89 orientation (socket empty, board on)

Using the original layout's U89 pin-1 mark, two holes at the **empty end** (the end towards J8) give opposite predictions under the two theories:

| Hole | Theory A: pin 1 at the empty end | Theory B: pin 1 at the other end | Measured |
|---|---|---|---|
| X = bottom-right (`captures/screen/probe_point_boot_socket_X.jpg`) | pin 1 = VCC | pin 15 = D3 (data) | **4.66 V steady** (`20261105-155203_bootsocket-holeX-5V.png`) |
| Y = bottom-left (`captures/screen/probe_point_boot_socket_Y.jpg`) | pin 28 = VCC | pin 14 = **GND** | **4.63 V steady** |

**Confirmed: socket U89 pin 1 is at the empty end (towards J8/SB), as the placement drawing shows.** A 24-pin chip correctly sits at the other end, which it did, with **its pin 1 facing the empty end**. The РФ5's triangle/bar marks faced away from it, so **if the triangle marks pin 1, the РФ5 was in backwards**. The CPU would then have run garbage, which matches the original symptom. The chip's pin-1 marking remains to be confirmed: read it on the T48 and compare the MD5.
- **Socket pin 23** (6th hole up the left column from Y; chip pin 21 = 2716 VPP / AT28C16 /WE): **steady 4.64 V**, min 4.48 V (`20261105-155440_bootsocket-pin23-VCC.png`). An address line (BA11 in SB 2732 mode, or A8/A9 if the count was off by one) would dip low every refresh cycle, because the Z80 drives I = 00h onto A8–A15 then. **So SB is in the 2716 position and pin 23 is tied to VCC: an AT28C16 in this socket is write-protected.** ✅
- **Correction:** the owner points out that the РФ5 has a **gold metallization key at the empty-end (Y side) corner**, opposite the printed ◄. If the gold key marks pin 1, the РФ5 was **correctly** inserted and the "reversed ROM" theory falls. Downgraded to uncertain. It will be settled by an AT28C16 holding the exact РФ5 image, inserted notch-towards-empty-end.
